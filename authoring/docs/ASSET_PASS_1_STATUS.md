## Asset by asset, honestly

### B05 — USB-C receptacle ✅
- Correct **obround (stadium) cross-section** — the shape that makes USB-C recognisable. Not a rounded rectangle.
- 8.94 × 3.16 mm outer, 8.34 × 2.56 mm cavity → uniform 0.30 mm wall, real front rim.
- **24 individually named contact objects** (`B05_PIN_A01`…`B12`), 12 per row, 0.50 mm pitch. Row B is mirrored so **A1 and B12 share an X position** — that is the reversibility lesson, built into the geometry.
- Six pin-group material slots (GND / VBUS / CC / D± / SBU / SS) so the runtime can light a whole signal group in one call.
- White LCP tongue. Real tongues are light plastic precisely so the gold contacts stand out — accurate *and* the teaching point.
- `ANIM_B05_EXPLODE` f1–90, reversible. Shell → housing → tongue+contacts, staggered so the pin row stays readable.
- `B05_STATE_BENT_PINS` with 3 deflected contacts.
- Brushed-steel shader with directional grain and a fold seam.

**Still to fix:** contacts read slightly dark inside the cavity (they're recessed under the shell overhang — needs a touch more bounce). Dimensions are USB-C spec nominal and **still need checking against a real handset receptacle datasheet** before sign-off.

### B40 — Solder joint states ✅ (the best result)
The five states are built as **real meniscus geometry**, not a roughness swap:
- **GOOD** — concave ellipse centred *outside* the solder, so the surface hollows inward. Low contact angle, fillet reaches full pad width.
- **COLD** — convex ellipse centred *in the corner*, so it balls up. Contact angle >90°, bare gold pad visible beyond it. Grainy shader with a noise bump.
- **CRACKED** — GOOD geometry displaced by a real 0.045 mm gap from the lead, with oxidised faces inside the fissure.
- **BRIDGED** — shiny, well-formed, spanning both pads. Deliberately *not* dirty — a bridge is a good joint in the wrong place, and most training material misses that.
- **DRY** — starved, no fillet, visible gap.

All five share one identical pad and component, rendered from one camera at one exposure — coverage measured identical at 21.1% across all five, so the learner compares the joint, not the scene. Plus a 5-across row render for a wall chart. **This is the one I'd print as a handout.**

### B02 — Mainboard ⚠️ usable, one pass short
Built: irregular outline with a battery notch, laminated edge (FR4 core + proud mask lip), 150 instanced passives on 2 shared meshes, ~200 plated vias as real pipes on a shared mesh, 3 shield cans (PMIC one removable for Fix 4/5), 18 gold test points, silkscreen designators as real geometry, a 7×7 BGA ball field under the PMIC, and pad footprints at all four zones that survive with the part removed — so the **bare pad vs cleaned pad** pair for Fix 2 step 5 works.

## The library is the real deliverable

`cg_lib.py` (43 KB) now encodes the house standard as working code, and every asset inherits it:

- **Units** — mm scale, 0.01 mm clip start. The single most common macro-scale setup bug, fixed once.
- **`auto_expose()`** — renders small, measures mean luminance over covered pixels, corrects in stops. At mm scale Blender's light falloff still treats units as metres, so hand-guessed energies render black. This removes the guesswork entirely.
- **`frame_camera()`** — closed-form solve. Builds the camera basis by hand and solves `dist = max(a + |u|/tan_h, a + |v|/tan_v)` over the real bounding box. My first two attempts failed: the bounding-*sphere* version over-estimated badly for flat wide parts, and the iterative fix read `matrix_world`, which Blender caches until a depsgraph update, so it diverged.
- **`studio_world()` + `reflector_cards()`** — metals reflect their environment; in a near-black world a perfect steel shader renders as a black blob. This matters more than any shader tweak.
- **`export_glb()`** — a `--python` script runs in a restricted context with no `active_object`, which the glTF exporter reads unconditionally. Builds a real UI override, and never lets an export failure kill the build.
- **`finish()`** — bevel + harden normals + smooth by angle on every object. An unbevelled 90° edge is the #1 tell of rushed work.
- **`obround` / `lathe` / `frustum` / `pipe` / `tube` / `plate`** — the primitives the whole consumables and connector set is made of.
- **`fcurves()`** — Blender 5.x replaced `action.fcurves` with slotted actions; this handles both.

Four assets and every remaining one on the list are ~200–400 lines each on top of this.

## Recommended next steps, in order

1. **B10 + B11 IFC assembly** — highest teaching value, and do the glTF round-trip rig gate *first*, before animating.
2. **B02 zone legibility** — drop B05 into the port footprint and B11 into the IFC footprint. The path becomes readable the moment real connectors sit on it, and both assets already exist.
3. **B28 cleanup** — reduce body bevel, re-pose the handpiece, decimate the hose. Half a day.
4. **Verify B05 against a real datasheet** and correct the dimensions. Proportion accuracy matters more on this asset than any other, because learners will hold the real part in tweezers and compare.
5. **The device gate** — view B05 and B02 at 720p on an actual sub-₹12,000 Android before signing off materials. Metal legibility behaves completely differently there than on a monitor.
